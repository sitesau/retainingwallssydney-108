Retaining Wall Builders Sydney Checklist Explained

A quote for retaining work is only as good as the briefing behind it. These eight checks are drawn from what the source guide says actually drives scope, price and approval, and none of them need a builder on site to complete.

  1. Measure the retained height. Get a tape or laser measure and record how much ground the wall must hold, not how tall you want the face to look.
  2. Record the length. Cost is driven by material and wall face area, which is height multiplied by length, so a quote without both figures is hard to compare.
  3. Describe the slope above and below. A wall cut into a hill carries different loading to one on flat ground with a small terrace.
  4. List nearby structures. Driveways, buildings, fences and upslope ground can all impose surcharge.
  5. Trace the water path. Note where rain currently collects and where stormwater connection sits.
  6. Flag the boundary position. Setbacks between the wall and property lines matter for approval.
  7. Photograph the existing wall. Bulging, cracking or leaning points to failure, not a repaint job.
  8. Write the suburb down. Sandstone country, shale clay pockets and sandy ground near Botany Bay each behave differently.

Understanding The 600mm Trigger And What Follows It

The approval question should be settled before design conversations, not after. The source guide is direct: exemption is not automatic. A wall qualifies as exempt only when it is 600mm or less and clears every other relevant Codes SEPP condition, including boundary setbacks, separation from other walls, drainage connection, distance from waterbodies and heritage constraints.

Once the wall exceeds that height, or once surcharge loads from a driveway, building, fence or slope enter the picture, the path changes. The structure is designed by a structural engineer against AS 4678 and then assessed either as complying development or through a council development application.

  • Ask each builder, in writing, whether they expect the job to sit under or over the trigger.
  • Ask whether surcharge applies at your address specifically.
  • Ask who carries the engineering and who manages the approval paperwork.

A builder who cannot answer these questions before pricing is guessing, and their price will reflect that guess later.

The right question is not which wall looks best from the kerb. It is which system suits your soil, load and drainage path. Ask what sits behind the visible face: post or footing depth, drainage aggregate, geofabric, ag pipe and the connection to site stormwater. A lower quote often reflects a wall priced as a face only, with the hidden side left vague.

Replacement jobs deserve extra scrutiny. If the previous wall failed because water had nowhere to go, a prettier rebuild on the same footing logic repeats the failure. Ask the builder what caused the old structure to lean or crack, and whether their proposal addresses that cause.

What A Complete Quote Should Itemise

A comparable tender reads like a scope of works, not a single line. The source text identifies the components that commonly get left out of cheap numbers. Use this list to test every quote you receive:

  • Excavation and spoil removal from site
  • Drainage aggregate, geofabric and ag drain behind the wall
  • Stormwater connection, including who performs it
  • Posts or footings and how they are set
  • Structural engineering against AS 4678 where triggered
  • CDC or council DA support where required
  • Reinstatement of nearby surfaces disturbed by the work

Where an existing wall is involved, also ask whether the proposal is assessment, partial repair or full replacement, and get the failed cause identified in writing. When every builder prices the same scope in the same order, the numbers start to mean something. When scopes differ, the cheapest figure usually just describes the least work.

Who This Checklist Is For

This angle suits Sydney owners of sloping blocks, ageing timber sleeper walls, garden terraces, low boundary retaining, driveway support and walls that may need replacement rather than cosmetic repair. It fits sandstone areas across the north shore and northern beaches, shale clay pockets through the inner west and western suburbs, and sandy bayside ground around Botany Bay, because ground type changes how a wall must be built.

It is less relevant where no soil is retained and the work is purely decorative. Even then, boundary position and after rain pooling are worth recording, because a wall that looks minor from the front can hold more load than expected at the rear.

  1. Walk the block and record the facts. Measure retained height and length, note slopes, nearby structures, boundary position, current material and any pooling after rain.
  2. Test the approval trigger. Confirm whether the wall is 600mm or lower and whether every Codes SEPP condition is cleared, or whether AS 4678 engineering and a CDC or council DA apply.
  3. Trace the drainage path. Work out where water enters the backfill, how it exits through ag drain and aggregate, and where it connects to site stormwater.
  4. Brief every builder identically. Hand each builder the same measurements, photos and scope so quotes can be compared line for line.
  5. Compare complete scopes, not totals. Check excavation, spoil, drainage, footings, engineering, approvals and reinstatement are itemised in every tender before weighing price.

Common questions

Does a retaining wall under 600mm always avoid approval in Sydney? No. The source guide says exemption applies only when the wall is 600mm or less and every other Codes SEPP condition is met, including boundary setbacks, separation from other walls, drainage connection, distance from waterbodies and heritage constraints. Any single unmet condition changes the pathway.

Which measurements matter most when requesting quotes? Retained height and wall length, because cost is driven by material and wall face area, which is height multiplied by length. Add the slope above and below, what sits near the wall, access for excavation and whether water pools after rain.

What should I ask about drainage before accepting a price? Ask what happens behind the face: ag drain, gravel, geofabric and the stormwater connection are all named in the source text as part of the wall system. Confirm who performs the connection and how trapped water will exit once backfill is closed.
